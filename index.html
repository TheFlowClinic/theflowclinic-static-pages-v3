The Flow Clinic is a clinical-psychologist-led therapy service. Every clinician on our panel is registered with the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI), and every client is matched to one of them on their stated preferences rather than on whoever happens to have a slot free.

Matching based on preference is the whole idea. Most services leave that to chance. We treat it as the thing to get right before anything else happens.

We are not a wellness app with a chat window. There is assessment, a formulation of what is actually keeping the problem going, a treatment plan you can see, and a qualified clinician accountable for it.

What changed

July 2025 — the Supreme Court made this a constitutional obligation

In Sukdeb Saha v. State of Andhra Pradesh (2025 INSC 893), the Court found a “legislative and regulatory vacuum” around student suicide prevention and issued fifteen binding guidelines that remain in force until Parliament legislates. They apply to all educational institutions across India: schools, colleges, universities, coaching centres, training institutes, residential academies and hostels, irrespective of board or affiliation.

Among them: a published mental health policy drawing on UMMEED, MANODARPAN and the National Suicide Prevention Strategy, reviewed annually. A qualified counsellor, psychologist or social worker with demonstrable child and adolescent training for any institution with 100 or more students. For institutions below 100, formal referral linkages with external mental health professionals. Trained teaching and non-teaching staff. Documented referral pathways. Visible helplines. An annual anonymised compliance report.

January 2026 — CBSE turned it into an affiliation condition

By circular dated 19 January 2026, CBSE amended Clause 2.4.12 of its Affiliation Bye-Laws, 2018. Every affiliated secondary and senior secondary school must appoint a Counselling and Wellness Teacher at a ratio of 1:500 for Classes IX–XII, plus a separate Career Counsellor, with specified qualifications and a mandatory 50-hour CBSE capacity-building programme. This sits inside the affiliation bye-laws. It is a condition of affiliation, not an advisory.

February 2026 — CBSE specified what schools must actually do, and how often

A further circular requires all teaching, non-teaching and administrative staff to be trained at least twice a year, by certified mental health professionals, covering psychological first aid, identification of warning signs, response to self-harm, and referral mechanisms. Schools must run regular parent sensitisation programmes, maintain anonymised records, and file an annual report of wellness interventions, student referrals, training sessions and mental health activity.

This is the circular most schools have not yet operationalised. It converts mental health from a hire into a recurring calendar and reporting obligation.

June 2026 onward — higher education is next, and the direction is already visible

The Supreme Court's National Task Force, chaired by Justice (Retd.) S. Ravindra Bhat, released its interim report on 8 June 2026 after surveying over 16 lakh respondents across 2,119 institutions. It found that student suicides are a structural and institutional failure, that no statutory framework exists to prevent them, and that existing provision is generic and reactive. Around 9% of surveyed students reported suicidal thoughts. The final report is due October 2026.

The UGC's draft guidelines for higher education institutions, prepared following Saha, propose dedicated Mental Health and Wellbeing Centres and a counsellor-to-student ratio of 1:100 — five times tighter than the school requirement.

The direction is settled. What is still open is whether your institution builds a system or files a form.

“We already have a counsellor”

Appointing a counsellor satisfies one line of a fifteen-point framework. Here is what we find when we audit institutions that consider the problem solved.

The counsellor is unsupervised. A recent graduate is the only person responsible for a suicidal fifteen-year-old, a child disclosing abuse, and a parent who does not want to hear about either. No supervisor, no second opinion, no protocol.

There is no ceiling on the role. Active suicidality, eating disorders, psychosis, disclosed abuse. Without a defined escalation route and someone qualified to escalate to, the counsellor either carries what they should not, or freezes.

Teachers see it first and are told nothing. What to look for, what to say, what never to say, whom to inform when they see children going through psychological issues. CBSE now requires this twice a year, from a certified professional.

Parents find out the wrong way. Nobody has told parents what the counsellor does, when a child might be seen without a parent present, or what stays confidential. So the first they hear of it is from their own child, and the call to the principal starts from suspicion. A school with no prepared answer usually protects itself by quietly limiting what the counsellor is allowed to do.

Nothing is written down safely. Notes in shared drives and WhatsApp groups are now a named liability under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 and the DPDP Act 2023.

None of this means your counsellor is doing a bad job. It means they were given a role without a system underneath it.

Does it work

We will tell you what the evidence supports and what it does not, because you will be pitched the opposite by others.

What the evidence supports

Gatekeeper training reliably improves staff knowledge, confidence and referral behaviour — teachers who are trained notice more and act sooner. Clinical supervision improves practitioner decision-making and reduces burnout and dropout in early-career clinicians. Matching clients to therapists on their stated preferences reduces premature dropout, which is the single largest failure mode in therapy. Documented escalation pathways reduce the time between disclosure and appropriate care.

What the evidence does not support

No provider can demonstrate that their programme prevented a suicide at your institution. Suicide is statistically rare and multiply determined; the studies are not powered to show it and the honest literature says so. Anyone who tells you their programme prevents student suicides is selling you something they cannot deliver, and you should discount everything else they say.

What we commit to measuring, and reporting to you

  • Baseline wellbeing survey at the start, repeated annually — you see movement, not assertion
  • Staff confidence and referral behaviour, before and after training
  • Referral volume, escalation volume, and time from identification to first clinical contact
  • Therapy utilisation, attendance and dropout rates
  • Training completion by staff category

If these numbers do not move, we will say so in the annual report and tell you what we think is wrong.

The clauses, in full

Clause 2.4.12.1 — Counselling and Wellness Teacher. Every CBSE-affiliated secondary and senior secondary school must appoint one regular Counselling and Wellness Teacher (socio-emotional counsellor) for up to every 500 students enrolled in Classes IX–XII.

Clause 2.4.12.2 — Career Counsellor. A separate, compulsory Career Counsellor appointment for Classes IX–XII, with its own qualification and ratio requirements.

Qualifications (either role). A graduate or postgraduate degree in Psychology (clinical, counselling, applied or educational); or a postgraduate degree in Social Work with specialisation in mental health or counselling; or any UG/PG degree plus a diploma in school counselling or child guidance and counselling.

Required competencies. SEL delivery, crisis intervention, identification of mental health concerns, parent and teacher sensitisation, and confidentiality.

Training requirement. A mandatory 50-hour CBSE-recognised capacity-building programme (CBP3). Where a Career Counsellor is not immediately available, the circular permits nominating a trained teacher who must acquire the listed qualifications and competencies within two academic years.

Hub-and-spoke provision for smaller schools. CBSE's counselling hub-and-spoke model may be adopted: larger, better-resourced hub schools support and share access with smaller nearby spoke schools.

Source: CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws, 2018, as amended by circular dated 19 January 2026.

About us

The Flow Clinic is a clinical psychology practice built around one question that most services skip: is this the right therapist for this person?

We are RCI-registered clinical psychologists working with individuals, schools, universities and workplaces. We match before we book, we keep the panel small, and we tell you what the work will involve before you commit to it.

Our vision

Finding the right therapist is of utmost importance. The working relationship between client and therapist influences the outcome of therapy massively. In short it determines whether the need for which therapy was sought was met with clear outcomes.

Most people in India who try therapy do so once. If that one attempt lands badly, the conclusion drawn is usually that therapy does not work, rather than that the fit was wrong. We want the second conclusion to be the obvious one, and we want the fit to be gettable on the first try.

Our vision is a country where finding the right therapist is a process, not luck.

Our mission

To match every client to a therapist on the things that actually shape the work: pace, structure, how much the therapist leads, how much the past comes into the room.

We do this with a validated preference instrument rather than a form and a waiting list, and we hold the panel small enough that we can stand behind each match we make.

Our philosophy

Good therapy isn't a volume business.

We could grow faster by signing every therapist who applies and letting them take unlimited clients. Most platforms do exactly that. We think it is why so many people try therapy once, get a poor fit, and never go back.

So we built the opposite. A small, vetted panel. Capped caseloads. A matching process that treats the first session as something to get right, not something to get through.

Fit before brand

Decades of outcome research point the same way. The working relationship between client and therapist predicts whether therapy helps more reliably than the school of therapy does, and clients whose stated preferences are accommodated are measurably less likely to drop out. We built the service around that finding instead of around a method we happen to like.

No promises of transformation. No thirty-day band-aid fixes. A clear account of the method, the likely course, and what progress will look like for you, said plainly enough that you can hold us to it.

You can read through the site, look at how matching works, and understand what a first session actually involves, so that when you bring it up you are offering something concrete rather than a vague suggestion to “see someone.” People are far more likely to say yes to a specific, low-commitment first step than to the idea of therapy in general.

You can point them towards the free 15 minute discovery call. It costs nothing, it is audio only, and it commits them to nothing further. For someone who is hesitant, that is often an easier yes than a first session.

You can also book a session for yourself. Supporting someone who is struggling is genuinely hard, and it is common to reach a point where you do not know what to say, how much to push, or how to keep your own life steady while you worry about theirs. That is worth talking through with someone, on its own terms.

A few things worth knowing

They have to choose it. Therapy works when the person walks in wanting something from it. You can open the door. You cannot walk them through it, and pushing usually produces a few resentful sessions and then a refusal to try again.

You cannot sit in on their sessions or ask us how they are doing. For adults, what happens in therapy stays between the client and their therapist. We will not confirm whether someone is a client, share what was discussed, or pass on messages. This is not us being difficult. It is the reason therapy works at all, and it would protect you in the same way if you were the one in the room.

If it is your child under 18, the consent and confidentiality arrangement is different, and you will be involved in a defined way. We will explain exactly how before anything begins.

If you are worried about immediate safety — if they have talked about ending their life, or you believe they are in danger right now — this is not the right route. Please contact a hospital emergency department, or call Tele-MANAS on 14416, which is free and available 24 hours in multiple Indian languages.

How to raise it with them

Lead with what you have actually seen rather than what you have concluded. “You have not been sleeping and you have stopped calling anyone back” is easier to hear than “I think you are depressed.” Offer the specific first step: a fifteen minute call, free, from their own phone. And leave room for no. The offer staying open matters more than them accepting it today.

FAQ

Is online therapy as effective as meeting a therapist in person?

Yes. Online therapy is the same treatment, delivered over video, and the research consistently finds comparable outcomes for the presentations we most commonly treat. What changes is the room, not the rigour: the same registered clinical psychologists, the same assessment, the same treatment plan, the same fifty-minute session, the same clinical notes.

The one genuine difference is that online therapy asks slightly more of you in turning up and taking it seriously, because nothing about your surroundings marks it out as an appointment. We build that into how we set things up with you from the first session.

How does the matching process work?

Before your first session you complete a short preference profile — about ten minutes. It asks how you want therapy to work: how directive you want your therapist to be, how much you want to work with emotion, whether you want to focus on the past or on the present, and whether you want warm support or focused challenge.

We match you to clinicians whose way of working fits your answers. You choose from what we put forward. We do not hand you a directory and wish you luck.

What if therapy didn't work for me before?

That is often a fit problem rather than a you problem. The working relationship between client and therapist is one of the largest predictors of whether therapy helps — larger than the specific school of therapy. Most people are matched by whoever happened to be free that week. We match on your stated preferences from the start, so you are not starting over with the wrong person.

What if my matched therapist is full?

It happens, because caseloads are capped. You can join the waitlist for that clinician and we will tell you the moment a slot opens, or you can see your next-best matches — other clinicians your profile fits well who are available sooner. Either way you are not left without care.

Is what I say confidential, and what happens to my data?

Sessions are confidential, with the standard clinical and legal limits set out for you in writing before you begin. Your records are handled in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017. We tell you what we collect, why we collect it, how long we keep it and how to have it deleted.

Do I need a diagnosis to start therapy?

No. You do not need a diagnosis, a referral or a crisis. Many people come because sleep, focus, irritability, a relationship or a decision is getting in the way, and they would rather deal with it now than after it gets worse.

What does a session cost, and what is your cancellation policy?

Fees are shown in full before you book — no packages you have to buy blind, no surprise charges. Sessions run fifty minutes. Our cancellation window and any applicable charge are stated in writing at the point of booking.
